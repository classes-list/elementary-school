📝 Homework & Study Expectations

Following the MSOS guidelines of ~10 minutes per grade, first graders should expect roughly 10–15 minutes of homework per night, plus daily independent reading. Homework is meant to reinforce (not introduce) what was learned in class.

  • Each day your child brings home their Home-to-School Folder and Home-to-School Agenda Book. Please check both the folder and initial the agenda book daily. Return to school each day.
  • Your child’s homework log for the week will be glued into their Home-to-School Agenda Book.
  • The homework log will indicate the homework each day and note important dates and reminders.
  • Homework sheets will go home in the folder (Monday–Thursday). Sheets will be double-sided with math and language arts work. No homework is assigned on Fridays or during school breaks.
  • Please remove all old paperwork daily. You will know I have checked homework if there is a star or happy face at the top.
  • Monthly calendar and lunch schedule are in sheet protectors — please leave these documents in the folder.

Students will also receive reading folders with books at their reading level, starting in September. Reading folders go home on Monday with books for the week. Books are to be returned in the reading folder at the end of the school week in order to get a new set on Monday.

To Support Homework at Home

  • Set a consistent, quiet study time and place.
  • Read the directions together, then step back and let your child work independently.
  • Encourage your child to do their best work.
  • If a task takes much longer than expected or your child is struggling, please let me know.

🤝 Classroom Procedures & Behavior Expectations

Our class has created a classroom contract that establishes how we’d like to feel while at school and the actions that make everyone feel like a part of our community. Students participated in a collaborative discussion in which we agreed upon the actions we wanted to see. Each action fell under the umbrella terms of safety, attentiveness, respect, kindness, and doing your best. Each student is expected to follow the agreed-upon expectations.

Other classroom expectations will be explicitly taught and demonstrated daily. Students are expected to work to high standards but are treated with warmth, respect, and fairness. I will contact you directly if your student is having difficulty following classroom expectations.

📊 Assessment & Grading

Daily class work and homework will be checked for thoroughness, effort, and neatness. Writing assessments and class projects will be graded using rubrics. I will frequently sit down with students one-on-one to formally assess their academic progress. Throughout the school day I use informal, ongoing assessments to track student growth, and summative assessments at the end of a unit or topic to measure growth. Report cards are given at the end of each quarter and are graded using a 1 to 4 scale.

  • 1 — Beginning: Beginning to understand the concept or skill; requires significant support and guidance; limited understanding or mastery.
  • 2 — Developing: Demonstrates partial understanding or skill; needs occasional assistance or support; progressing but with notable gaps.
  • 3 — Proficient: Meets grade-level expectations; demonstrates solid understanding of concepts or skills; works independently with minimal errors.
  • 4 — Mastery: Exceeds grade-level expectations; demonstrates thorough understanding and can apply skills in new situations; work is consistently accurate and shows higher-level thinking.

Report cards are based on how your child performs each quarter. Grades are not reflected based on year-end benchmarks. Students also take a standardized assessment called STAR for early literacy and math in August and May.

🏡 How to Support Your Child at Home

Families are our most important partners in helping students succeed. The routines and habits established at home provide the foundation for strong first-grade learners and make a meaningful difference in your child’s learning, confidence, and overall success.

Children thrive when they have a consistent bedtime, arrive at school on time, read with an adult each day, complete homework, and talk with their family about their school day. Encouraging independence and responsibility through simple daily tasks — such as packing their backpack, caring for their belongings, and taking ownership of their work — helps build confidence and self-reliance.

Equally important is maintaining open communication with your child’s teacher, creating a strong home-school partnership that supports every child’s growth. By working together to establish these routines, foster responsibility, and stay connected, we can help every first grader become a confident, capable, and successful learner.
