💜 Mrs. Parone's Teaching Philosophy

My teaching philosophy for Kindergarten 2 is rooted in nurturing the development of the whole child — socially, emotionally, spiritually, and academically. Kindergarten is a year of enormous growth: five- and six-year-olds thrive in a warm, structured environment where they feel safe to be curious, safe to try, and safe to make mistakes.

I believe learning at this age happens best through play, song, movement, story, and hands-on exploration. Every day in K2 blends structured academic work with joyful discovery, so that our children build strong foundations in reading, writing, and math while also learning how to be kind classmates, patient friends, and prayerful children of God.

At Mary, Star of the Sea School, we guide our students to become S.T.A.R.s:

  • ⭐ Self-aware, Confident Individuals
  • ⭐ Teachable, Lifelong Independent Learners
  • ⭐ Active Christians
  • ⭐ Responsible Citizens

Together, we will nurture hearts and minds, unite in purpose and spirit, and inspire one another each day — empowered to succeed, and always guided by faith.

📊 Grading

I check daily classwork for effort, thoroughness, and neatness. Any projects will be graded using a rubric. Academic progress is measured through one-on-one assessment: informal assessments happen throughout each day, and summative assessments are given at the end of each unit to track student progress.

Report cards are sent home at the end of each quarter. Student performance is graded on a 1–4 scale. Report cards reflect how each student performed each quarter — grades are not based on year-end assessments.

Primary Grades K-3 Report Card Grading:

1
Beginning
  • The student is beginning to understand the concept
  • Requires a significant amount of support
  • Limited understanding of topic
2
Developing
  • The student shows some understanding of a topic
  • Needs some support
3
Proficient
  • The student meets grade level expectations
  • Demonstrates solid understanding of topic
  • Works independently with minimal support
4
Mastery
  • The student exceeds grade level expectations
  • Demonstrates a thorough understanding and can apply the topic to new situations
  • Work is consistently accurate and shows a higher level of thinking

Kindergartners also take the STAR Early Literacy assessment in fall, winter, and spring so we can track growth in phonics and reading. English Language Learners take the WIDA assessment as required.

📚 Kindergarten Curriculum

Full quarter-by-quarter arc below. Materials and topics are drawn from our K2 pacing guide.

📖 Language Arts — Sight Words, Phonics, Reading & Writing, Handwriting

Sight Words: the first 66 words from the Fry List (aligned with Sounds of Spelling).

Phonics · Q1
Student names; letters Mm, Ss, Aa, Rr, Tt, Ll, Nn, Bb, Oo, Pp, Cc, Ii, Ff, Dd, Hh
Phonics · Q2
Letters Uu, Gg, Kk, Ee, Vv, Jj, Ww, Yy, Qq, Zz · short vowels a and i · CVC words with e/o/u · CVC digraphs sh, th, ch, wh
Phonics · Q3
Digraphs qu, ck · CVC word families -at, -an, -am, -ap, -op, -ot, -ob, -im, -ip, -in, -ill, -ug, -ut, -ed, -et
Phonics · Q4
Plural nouns · inflectional endings with -s · initial s-blends (st, sp, sm, sn, sw, sc, sk, sl) · initial blends (bl, cl, fl, gl, pl) · initial r-blends (br, cr, dr, fr, gr, pr, tr) · long a, e, i, o, u · CVC words with long a, i, u, o
Reading & Writing
Q1 exploring letters and sounds, forming words, composing stories · Q2 developing reading powers, writing a personal story · Q3 pattern power, writing our opinion · Q4 becoming avid readers and writing about a topic
Handwriting
Zaner-Bloser Handwriting Book · basic strokes · proper pencil grip and posture · upper- and lowercase letter formation · writing numerals · applying handwriting skills to classroom and home life
